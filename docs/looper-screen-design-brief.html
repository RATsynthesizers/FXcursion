what changed between the two pictures, and what deliberately did not

The separators went grey. At 1× they bound the loop and the window, because the loop fills the span exactly. Zoomed, they can only be one of the two — the window edges — so their colour says which. Two setColor calls on a zoom step, nothing per frame.

The cursor did not move. It is the anchor: the column under it stays put and the take stretches around it. That is why the menu encoder is worth spending on the cursor — one knob gives position at every magnification, and scrolling falls out of it when the cursor reaches an edge.

The ruler thinned out. Bars still stick to the loop, so at 4× only the boundaries that fall inside the window are drawn. The grid coarsens with it — same 1/4, four cells instead of sixteen, because a cell is a quarter of a bar and there is now a quarter of a bar less on screen per pixel.

Everything else is identical. Same bands, same type, same 288 px span. Zoom changes what the pixel buffer contains, not what any widget is or where it sits.

The type rule I got wrong

PixelizerBold 16 is the smallest face in the build. Its line box is 20 px and a digit advances 8 px — 0 is 8 wide, 1 is 6, ten rows tall. Size 22 is a 28 px box.

Two consequences that kill the earlier layout:

  • A 20 px band holds exactly one line with zero padding. Label above value is two boxes — 40 px minimum. That is why every readout here is one line, label left dim, value right bright.
  • A full-width line is ~40 characters at size 16, ~29 at size 22. START 0:01 CUR 0:04 STOP 0:11 is 31. It fits; a fourth field would not.

Bands are 26 px, not 20: one 20 px box plus 3 px above and below. The 6 px value bar sits inside that padding, full-column width, which is the only reason a proportional indicator is legible at this size at all — it is 296 px long, not 20 px tall.

Fixed separators — the span is 288

The separators do not move. They sit at x=12 and x=304, 4 px wide, and the loop is always everything between them: x=16 to x=304, 288 px. Trimming start or stop does not slide a marker — it rescales what is drawn inside that fixed span.

288 is chosen, not left over. It divides by 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 9, 12 and 16, so every plausible bar count gives an integer bar width and every tick lands on a whole pixel:

One moving thing

Fixed separators collapse the layer model. Nothing is "outside the selection" any more, so the selection shading disappears — and the separators, being static, are drawn once and never invalidated again.

Baked if it changes on a detent, overlaid if it changes on a frame. Grid, bars and envelope are one rebuild of the same buffer — a few hundred microseconds off the cached peak table, one push, on a knob turn. The cursor is the only thing that moves at frame rate, and it costs a 4 px column.

what trimming looks like now

Because the display always normalises to the fixed span, moving start or stop does not move anything on screen — the envelope reshapes and the numbers change. That is why the START · CUR · STOP line is not decoration: with the separators pinned, it is the only statement of where you actually are in the take.

Dropping zoom cost nothing, incidentally: the rebuild path had to exist for BARS, GRID and trimming regardless.

Grid and bar contrast

The previous values were unreadable and you were right to say so. Five units between the alternating greys is invisible on this panel. Corrected:

The grid goes from 5 to 14 units apart — enough to read as alternating blocks at arm's length, still far enough below the envelope that it never competes with it. Bar ticks go from 84 units of separation to 162, on their own near-black ruler ground, so they read as structure rather than as more grid.

why shading for grid and lines for bars

At 1/16 a grid of lines is 15 hairlines fighting the envelope for the same pixels. Alternating ground reads as density instead of clutter, needs no minimum spacing, and degrades gracefully — at 1/32 it becomes texture rather than noise.

Bar ticks are the exception: there are only BARS−1 of them, they are structural, and the 20 px ruler gives them their own ground so they never cross the waveform. Two different jobs, two different treatments — which is also why raising the bar contrast does not force raising the grid's.

zoom — and what the separators mean once you use it

UP and DOWN magnify horizontally, anchored on the cursor: the column under the cursor stays put and the take stretches around it. FUNC distinguishes them from undo and redo, so no mode is needed.

There is one consequence worth deciding rather than discovering. At 1× the separators bound the loop and the window, because the loop fills the span exactly. Zoomed in they can only be one of the two — the window edges — and the loop now continues past them.

So colour the separators by state: blue at 1×, where they are true loop bounds; dim grey above 1×, where they are only the edges of what you are looking at. They are already static Boxes that never invalidate, so a state colour costs two setColor calls on a zoom step and nothing at all per frame. Bar ticks keep working unchanged — the ruler simply shows whichever bar boundaries fall inside the window.

grid lock is the coarse mode

A step is always ±1. Raw, a 20 s loop is 320 detents end to end — 16 revolutions. Grid-locked to bars at 120 BPM it is 10 detents, and every marker lands on a fixed pixel column, which keeps its redraw rect even-aligned for free. Ship locked first; free movement is the special case, not the default.

Files — YES

Same five bands, same type rule. The list is the encoder-driven scroller this codebase already has: ScrollWheelWithSelectionStyle with DrawableListItems pools, snapping one item per detent — copy EffectListContainer, do not build a new one.

  • MENU scrolls, YES loads, FUNC returns.
  • First row is CREATE NEW, auto-named. The spool already writes loop%u.wav and accepts a null name, so create is one press and no naming UI.
  • Grey out load while a save is queued — the spool refuses it rather than queueing, so a grey control tells the truth.
one correction to inherit

The existing wheel sits at x=45, which is odd. The display push rounds x down and the right edge up to even, so every scroll step is silently widened. Place the file wheel on even bounds — x=40 w=240, or full width.

Nothing in the application can list the card yet — the spool has acquire, commit, load and busy, but no enumerate. FatFs has f_opendir/f_readdir compiled in. Run the scan on the spool thread and cache it; a load blocks for seconds.

Drawing

The waveform is a PixelDataWidget over your own RGB565 buffer — 320×122 is 78 080 B in RAM_D2. A partial invalidate blits only the sub-rect, so a 6×122 column is 1 464 B, and nothing is re-rasterised.

  • setX, then one invalidateRect spanning old to new. moveTo invalidates twice.
  • Invalidate only when the integer column changes. At 1× the cursor advances 0.27 px per frame — three frames in four cost nothing.
  • Accumulate the tick's encoder events, move once. The queue drains 8 per frame; per-event moves cost 8 pushes and 8 TE waits.
  • Markers 2 px wide at even x. The push rounds to even anyway — take the width free.
no canvas buffer on this screen

CANVAS_BUFFER_SIZE exists only in EffectSettingsViewBase and SystemViewBase, and setup/reset are static, called in those screens' constructor and destructor. LooperViewBase declares none — so a Circle, Line or Shape here renders nothing, silently. Everything above is a Box, a text area, or your own pixels.

Where the peaks come from

This used to be the one place the looper was worse off than the recorder. It no longer is: the frame now carries the looper live, so this board gets the samples the same way the recorder's arrive, and the rule collapses to one line.

The audio comes to you. Decimate it as it lands.

slots 5..8   four looper planes, continuous, 768 kB/s
slot  9      sel:1 | pos:23 | gen:4 | state:4, loopers alternating

No peak-table transfer, no new format enum, no 400 ms over the control link. A fresh take, an overdub, a loop loaded from the card — all three arrive the same way, and the interface never has to ask for anything twice.

Sizing the table

Buckets are set by how far you want to zoom, not by loop length: buckets = 288 × maxZoom. Min and max per bucket as int8, per channel.

zoombucketsms / columnstereo table
1×28869.41 152 B
8×2 3048.79 216 B
32×9 2162.236 864 B

32× for 37 KB is the sweet spot — a column is 2.2 ms, finer than any trim needs, and past that the numeric readout is the precision anyway. int8 is not a compromise: the band gives about 52 px per lane, so ±26 px of travel against int8's ±127, five times finer than the display can resolve at any zoom.

cap zoom at the table, visibly

Beyond 32× a column would hold fewer frames than a bucket, so zoom must stop — and stop by refusing the UP press rather than magnifying a lie. The table's resolution is the honest limit of what the screen knows.

The two buffers, and which memory each wants

Two stages, and they want opposite things:

BufferWritten bySizeWhere
live half, double-bufferedMDMA4 096 BRAM_D2 — non-cacheable, DMA-reachable
peak tables, 2 loopersCPU73 728 BRAM_D1 — cacheable, and caching helps

The live destination need only survive until the decimation runs, and that happens once per half — 128 frames × 4 planes × 4 B, double-buffered, is 4 096 bytes. Not a mirror of the take: a window one half wide.

I had this backwards a revision ago. When the table was going to arrive over SPI it had to be MDMA-writable and therefore non-cacheable; now the MDMA delivers audio and the CPU builds the table, so the table goes back to D1 where the cache is an advantage. The rule is just: whatever DMA touches goes in D2, whatever only the CPU touches goes in D1.

what the status slot buys, concretely

Without pos, a live sample cannot be placed — the take is a ring at a moving head, so sample N belongs to an unknown offset. Without gen, an UNDO or a CLEAR on the audio side rewrites the take and this board goes on drawing peaks that stopped being true, silently, until the playhead happens to re-cross every bucket.

Compare the generation counter every frame and invalidate the table when it moves. That is one compare per frame against a word already in the receive ring.

Sharing with the recorder

The two screens are never open at once, and that is worth cashing in. The two blocks are a different matter — BLOCK_RECORDER and BLOCK_LOOPER are placed independently in a chain and can sit either side of the effects, so nothing about the audio path may assume they are exclusive.

ResourceShared?Why
SPI frame slotsno — both permanentslots 1–4 and 5–8 tap different points in the chain and carry different audio. There is no arrangement that could share them
loop file slots 10–31already exclusiveFxLoop runs one session at a time; a second OPEN returns BUSY. Nothing to arrange
LoopSpool slotsuntouched by eitherno peak transfer exists any more — the audio arrives on the live planes
84 480 B pixel bandyes — one bufferwhichever screen is up owns it; 73 KB saved outright
peak tablesnoeach builds its own locally, from data it already has

The pixel band is the real win. Declare it once outside both views — a screen transition destroys the view, not the buffer — and the recorder's 320×114 band is simply the top 72 960 bytes of the looper's 320×132. Sizing the shared allocation to the larger of the two costs nothing and removes the duplicate entirely.

the cursor is also the scroll control

Zoomed in, the window shows part of the take and something has to move it. Nothing new is needed: the window follows the cursor. When the cursor comes within a few pixels of a separator, the window slides to keep it in view.

That is the payoff for spending the menu encoder on the cursor rather than on zoom. One knob gives position at every magnification, scrolling falls out of it for free, and there is no fifth control to find room for.

Undo already exists

LoopMem_Snapshot(nLooper, nFrames, bRestore) is written, tested, and has zero callers. Each bank's 11 MiB holds two plane-copies per channel precisely so the second can be history — 5.76 MB of take and the same again of undo.

  • The wire has no verb: PROTO_TRANSPORT_ACT stops at CLEAR 4. Undo and redo are values 5 and 6, purely additive, carried by the existing CtrlLinkIf_Transport.
  • It copies, so restoring destroys what you undid. With exactly two copies the primitive you want is a swap — undo exchanges take and undo, redo exchanges back. One level with redo free, no extra memory, and a pointer exchange instead of a 5.7 MB copy.

Order

  1. Five bands in Designer, waveform a placeholder Box.
  2. PixelDataWidget with a flat length bar and cursor — what the band degrades to anyway.
  3. Markers and the time line. This is where the frame budget is won.
  4. Bars and grid, locked by default.
  5. File browser on YES.
  6. Undo swap, then peaks.
