Recorder screen

FXcursion interface · ILI9341 320×240 · TouchGFX 4.25 · PixelizerBold 16 / 22

One recorder, reached through the grid — the same way one looper is. So this is not a mixer view of four channels; it is the looper screen's twin, with a take that grows instead of a loop that repeats.

Same five bands, same type rule, same 288 px span, same controls. Everything below is the delta.

the thing that makes this screen possible

The looper has no envelope on the wire, so its band is flat until peaks exist. The recorder needs nothing from the wire at all — the interface already owns the audio.

recorder[4][262144] lives in SDRAM on this controller: 4 MiB, 5.46 s per plane, written by MDMA and read by the card writer. A live waveform is a decimation of memory this board already has. No new telemetry, no protocol change, no peak field to add.

Nothing needed inventing. A recorder is a looper whose length is the card rather than the RAM, so the same five knobs mean the same five things — and two of them simply have nothing to act on until you stop.

inert, not hidden

Trim is meaningless mid-take, but the fields should stay on screen dimmed rather than disappear. Hiding them reflows the band and repaints everything; dimming is a colour change on two labels. It also keeps the two screens visually identical, which is the point of building them as twins.

The growing waveform

Same layer rule as the looper — baked if it changes on a detent, overlaid if it changes on a frame — with one addition: the take gets longer while you watch.

LayerChanges whenCost
grid ground, bar rulerBARS / GRID detentrebuild, 1 rect
envelope up to the headas it recordsappend only
separatorsnever0
write headevery frame4 px column
level stripevery framedelta column, 1–2 rects

Append, do not rebuild. A new column of envelope is one decimation of the newest ring samples written into the buffer at the head position, then one narrow invalidate. Nothing already drawn is recomputed. At 1× with a 5.46 s window across 288 px, a column is 19 ms of audio — about one new column every frame at 60 Hz, and the head and the column are adjacent so they union into a single rect.

The full rebuild path still exists, but only three things call it: a BARS or GRID change, a zoom step, and a trim. None of them happen while your hands are on the guitar.

what happens past 5.46 seconds

The ring holds 5.46 s per plane and a take is longer than that, so the screen cannot show the whole take from memory. Two honest options, and the choice is yours:

Scroll — the window is the last 5.46 s and the head sits at the right separator, audio flowing right to left. Truthful about what the board can still see, and it is what a tape machine does.

Accumulate — keep a second, much coarser envelope of the whole take in .bss, appended as the ring drains to the card, and show all of it. At one min/max pair per 1024 samples that is 2 B per 21 ms, so a ten-minute take costs about 56 KB. This is the better answer if you ever want to trim a long take on the device, and it is cheap.

No peak transfer, ever

The looper needs a peak table shipped over SPI because a freshly recorded loop lives only in audio SDRAM. The recorder never does, and the reason is structural rather than lucky: every sample of a recorder take crosses this controller by design, because writing it to the card is what the recorder is.

RangeSourceWire traffic
last 5.46 srecorder[4][262144], read directlynone
the whole takecoarse envelope appended as the ring drains to the cardnone

The accumulate option is not a second pass over anything — the writer already walks every chunk on its way to f_write, so a min/max per bucket rides along on a read that is happening regardless. At one pair per 1024 samples a ten-minute take is 55 KB, and it is CPU-written only, so unlike the looper's table it can live in RAM_D1 where caching helps.

what the two screens share, and what they do not

They are never open together, so the 84 480 B pixel band is one buffer, declared outside both views — a screen transition destroys the view, not the buffer. The recorder's 320×114 band is the top 72 960 bytes of the looper's 320×132; size the shared allocation to the larger and the duplicate disappears.

Nothing else needs arranging. The SPI loop slots are already exclusive — FxLoop permits one session at a time — and neither screen takes a LoopSpool slot for peaks: the looper arms its transfer straight at its table, and the recorder has no transfer to make.

Level strip

Eight pixels, one lane for mono, two of four for stereo, on the same 288 px span. It exists because the envelope shows history and setting input gain needs now.

  • aPeak[n] from telemetry, 0..65535 → 288 px. The only thing on this screen that needs the wire.
  • Fill blue, the segment above −6 dBFS amber. Two Box widgets, not a gradient.
  • setWidth, then one invalidateRect spanning old to new — a bare store plus a narrow rect, the same trick as the cursor. A 10 px move is 160 B, not 4 608.
  • Quantise to even pixels before comparing; the push rounds x to even anyway, so a change that lands on the same column should produce no rect.
  • Clip latches — once red, it stays until the next take or a FUNC press. A peak you missed is worse than no peak meter.
DROP is the most valuable field on this screen

nRecDropped means a staging half was not free and audio was lost. Show it as DROP n, amber above zero. A take with dropped blocks is unrecoverable and the only moment that information is actionable is while recording.

Recorder_BacklogChunks() is the early warning: the ring absorbs 5.46 s of card stall, so a thin bar that only appears past a quarter full says "the card is struggling" before anything is lost. Hidden below the threshold it costs nothing and never becomes wallpaper.

No canvas, no new widgets

Everything here is Box, BoxWithBorder, TextAreaWithWildcard and one PixelDataWidget — 320×114 RGB565, 72 960 B in RAM_D2, appended rather than rebuilt.

the trap, unchanged

CANVAS_BUFFER_SIZE exists only in EffectSettingsViewBase and SystemViewBase, and setup/reset are static, called in those screens' constructor and destructor. RecorderViewBase declares none — a Circle, Line or Shape here renders nothing, silently.

Order

  1. Clone the looper screen in Designer and change the two text bands. They are twins; build them that way rather than from scratch.
  2. Level strip from aPeak[], delta-column invalidate, even-pixel quantiser.
  3. Append-only envelope from the local SDRAM ring. No wire work, no protocol change.
  4. FOOT transport, elapsed counter, and DROP.
  5. Decide scroll vs accumulate past 5.46 s — the only real open question here.
  6. Trim and file browser, both shared with the looper.
